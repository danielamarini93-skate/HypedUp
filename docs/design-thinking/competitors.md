## Table stakes (what users will expect)
- Phone and a fast way to ask (WhatsApp/call/form) visible on mobile at all times
- Clear service list with a page per key service
- Reviews and star rating near the top
- Product brands they recognise (Ghost, MetaTrak, Scorpion, Thinkware, CarPlay)
- Location, opening hours, map
- Some price guidance (the e-commerce competitor sets the expectation that prices exist)

## Opportunities (gaps Hyped Up can own)
1. **Personality.** Nobody in security/electronics looks or sounds human and creative. Hyped Up can be the workshop with a face (Hypa), colour and graffiti, without losing credibility.
2. **Electronics + styling under one roof, made personal.** Competitors split into "security shop" or "wrap shop". "We adapt the car to you" is unclaimed.
3. **Proof through real builds.** Competitors show stock-like product shots; Hyped Up has real cars, real bikes and video.
4. **Answer-shaped service pages.** Most competitors have little crawlable text (Creative FX has no H1). Clear pages with price, FAQ and schema can win local search and AI answers.
5. **Car-specific pages** (the CarPlay-by-brand pattern): "CarPlay for BMW / Mini / Mercedes", "Ghost for Range Rover". High intent, low competition locally.
6. **Culture and community** (Bike Shed pattern): builds, meets, merch, show cars (Hypa's red Mini, the 1 Series build). Gives enthusiasts a reason to follow and return.
7. **Collabs:** b:spoke's artist wrap collab shows the "art and craft" angle sells. Hyped Up's mural and graffiti fit naturally.

## Avoid
- Mega-menus with dozens of links (Car Keys, Car Sound): overwhelming on mobile
- Cookie banners covering the main action on first load (every UK site checked does this)
- Parts-shop feel: product grids without people or work
- Cinematic-but-empty heroes with no text (bad for SEO and accessibility)
- Self-awarded claims ("most trusted", "premier") without proof

## Next steps (ranked)

3. Add reviews (Google) high on the homepage; ask Hypa for permission to use them
