**What it means for Hyped Up** (recommendations)
- **Same promise, so say it better:** they also claim "one-stop shop" for performance + styling. Our edge is *electronics + security + styling*, a named person (Hypa) and clear "from £" prices; none of which they show. Keep "one-stop" as a supporting phrase, not the headline.
- **Fame and films work:** Pimp My Ride and YouTube builds are their strongest trust signal. Equivalent for us: a **Builds** section with short real videos and "featured on / partner of" proof (Air Lift dealer, Ghost installer) near the action, not hidden in the footer.
- **Do better above the fold:** they have no action in the hero; ours has one clear WhatsApp quote button.
- **Borrow:** the 3-step "how it works" (choose → drop off → collect), plain explanations of legal limits (tint %, MOT/emissions), lifetime/warranty lines (only if Hypa confirms ours), film/product brand names.
- **Avoid:** the wall of red legal text (put legal notes inline on the relevant service, calm grey), generic filler copy, empty photo tiles, contact forms with puzzles.
- **Legal notes we need too** (Hypa to confirm wording): front side windows 70% VLT and windscreen 75%; remaps/exhaust can affect MOT and insurance. Links to the open "tinted lights" clash in `friend-concept.md`.

## Table stakes (what users will expect)
- Phone and a fast way to ask (WhatsApp/call/form) visible on mobile at all times
- Clear service list with a page per key service
- Reviews and star rating near the top
- Product brands they recognise (Ghost, MetaTrak, Scorpion, Thinkware, CarPlay)
- Location, opening hours, map
- Some price guidance (the e-commerce competitor sets the expectation that prices exist)

## Opportunities (gaps Hyped Up can own)
1. **Personality.** Nobody in security/electronics looks or sounds human and creative. Hyped Up can be the workshop with a face (Hypa), colour and graffiti, without losing credibility.
2. **Electronics + styling under one roof, made personal.** Competitors split into "security shop" or "wrap shop". "We adapt the car to you" is unclaimed.
3. **Proof through real builds.** Competitors show stock-like product shots; Hyped Up has real cars, real bikes and video.
4. **Answer-shaped service pages.** Most competitors have little crawlable text (Creative FX has no H1). Clear pages with price, FAQ and schema can win local search and AI answers.
5. **Car-specific pages** (the CarPlay-by-brand pattern): "CarPlay for BMW / Mini / Mercedes", "Ghost for Range Rover". High intent, low competition locally.
6. **Culture and community** (Bike Shed pattern): builds, meets, merch, show cars (Hypa's red Mini, the 1 Series build). Gives enthusiasts a reason to follow and return.
7. **Collabs:** b:spoke's artist wrap collab shows the "art and craft" angle sells. Hyped Up's mural and graffiti fit naturally.

## Avoid
- Mega-menus with dozens of links (Car Keys, Car Sound): overwhelming on mobile
- Cookie banners covering the main action on first load (every UK site checked does this)
- Parts-shop feel: product grids without people or work
- Cinematic-but-empty heroes with no text (bad for SEO and accessibility)
- Self-awarded claims ("most trusted", "premier") without proof

## Next steps (ranked)
1. Make "personality + proof" the core of the homepage concept
2. Plan service pages, plus car-specific pages for CarPlay and Ghost (strategy v0.2)
3. Add reviews (Google) high on the homepage; ask Hypa for permission to use them
4. Consider a community/builds section and a collab story (mural, artists)

**Takeaways**
- Local service shops with **short menus** (Polimax, Bike Shed) read as more premium than the mega-menu shops.
- The mega-menu shops get their SEO from **make/model and brand pages**: Car Keys lists makes in the menu; we can
  get the same benefit with those pages kept **out of the menu** and linked from service pages and the footer.
- Every footer repeats **contact + hours + address**; the best (Bike Shed) groups links into 3–4 clear columns.
- Polimax puts **WhatsApp and call in the header**: confirms our one-tap action.
