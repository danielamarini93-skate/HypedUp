**How Plate Cloning Actually Works**

It's simpler than you'd think, which is the scary part.

*Step 1: Criminal finds a car similar to theirs. Same make, same model, same colour. They note down the registration number.*

*Step 2: They order plates online from a seller who doesn't check documents. No V5C required. No photo ID. Just type in the registration and pay.*

*Step 3: They fit the cloned plates to their car. Now their car appears to be YOUR car on every camera in the country.*

*Step 4: They drive however they want. Speed cameras, ULEZ zones, congestion charges, parking restrictions — none of it matters to them because every fine goes to YOU.*

Step 5: You get a letter through the door. Congestion charge for a journey you never made. Speeding ticket for a road you've never been on. Parking fine for a car park you've never visited.

And then the nightmare begins. Because YOU have to prove it wasn't you. The burden of proof falls on the innocent driver. You have to appeal every single fine, provide evidence of where your car actually was, and deal with the stress and admin of clearing your name.

Some people have received dozens of fines before they even realised what was happening.

How To Know If YOUR Plate Has Been Cloned

Here are the warning signs:

You receive fines for places you've never been. Congestion charge letters for Central London when you were in Bermondsey. Parking tickets for streets you've never heard of. Speeding tickets for roads you don't use.

You get a ULEZ charge notice but you haven't entered the zone. If your car is ULEZ compliant but you're getting charged, someone with a non-compliant vehicle is using your plate.

Your insurance company contacts you about an accident you weren't in. If a cloned vehicle is involved in an accident, your registration comes up on the system.

Police contact you about your vehicle being spotted somewhere suspicious. ANPR cameras flag your plate at a location you've never been to.

Your MOT history shows mileage discrepancies. If the cloned car goes through MOT with your registration, the mileage won't match yours.

You notice your car insurance premium suddenly increases. If the cloned vehicle racks up offences, it can affect your insurance record.

If ANY of these happen to you, act immediately. Don't ignore it. Don't assume it'll sort itself out. The longer a cloned plate is in use, the more damage it does to your record.

What To Do If Your Plate Has Been Cloned

Step 1: Report it to the police immediately. Call 101 or visit your local station. They'll put a marker on the Police National Computer to flag your vehicle as potentially cloned.

Step 2: Contact DVLA. They can issue you a new registration number free of charge if there's evidence of cloning. You'll need to pay for the new plates to be made, but the registration change itself is free.

Step 3: Appeal every single fine. Contact the issuing authority (TfL, local council, etc.) with evidence that your car was elsewhere. This is where a GPS tracker and dash cam become invaluable — they prove exactly where your car was at any given time.

Step 4: Report to Action Fraud. Call 0300 123 2040 or report online. This generates a case number that helps with fine appeals.

Step 5: Inform your insurance company. Let them know your plate has been cloned so any incidents involving the cloned vehicle don't affect your record.

Step 6: Consider getting a private registration. A unique private plate is harder to clone because the criminal needs to find a matching car AND registration. It's not foolproof, but it adds a layer of protection.

Common Questions About Number Plate Cloning

Q: How common is plate cloning in London?

A: Very. Over 11,000 cases were reported nationally in 2025, with London being the worst affected area due to ULEZ, congestion charges, and the density of ANPR cameras. The real figure is estimated to be 3-4 times higher.

Q: Can I tell if my plate has been cloned just by looking at my car?

A: No. The cloned plate is on someone else's car. Your car looks completely normal. You only find out when fines, charges, or police enquiries start arriving for things you didn't do.

Q: Will getting a private plate stop cloning?

A: It makes it harder but not impossible. A unique registration is more difficult to match to a similar vehicle, but determined criminals can still clone any plate. It's one layer of protection, not a guarantee.

Q: If I get fined because of a cloned plate, do I have to pay?

A: No, but you have to appeal. The burden of proof unfortunately falls on you to demonstrate that your vehicle wasn't at the location. This is where GPS tracker data and dash cam footage are invaluable — they provide irrefutable proof of your car's actual location.

Q: Is it illegal to make plates without checking documents?

A: Yes. The law requires that plates showing valid UK registration formats can only be supplied with proper documentation. But enforcement is weak and many sellers, particularly online, ignore this requirement entirely.

Q: What documents should a legitimate plate maker check?

A: V5C registration certificate (logbook), photo ID (driving licence or passport), and ideally DVLA database verification. At Hyped Up, we check all three. Every time. No exceptions.

Q: Can DVLA give me a new registration if mine is cloned?

A: Yes. If there's evidence of cloning, DVLA can issue a replacement registration free of charge. You'll pay for the new plates to be made, but the registration swap itself costs nothing.

Q: Does plate cloning affect my insurance?

A: It can. If offences are recorded against your registration, insurers may see them. That's why reporting cloning immediately and getting it flagged on the Police National Computer is crucial — it creates an official record that protects your insurance status.
