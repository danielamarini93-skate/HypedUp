Do Dash Cams Record When The Car Is Off? (Parking Mode Explained)

This is the second most asked question about dash cams. And the answer is: yes, but only if set up correctly.

Parking mode is a feature where the camera continues monitoring your car when the engine is off. There are two types:

Motion detection parking mode: Camera sleeps until it detects movement near the car. Then it starts recording. Captures people approaching, hit-and-runs, break-ins, vandalism.

Impact detection parking mode: Camera sleeps until the built-in G-sensor detects a bump or impact. Then it records the seconds before and after the impact. Captures someone hitting your parked car.

24/7 continuous parking mode: Camera records continuously, even when parked. Uses the most power but captures everything.

The critical requirement: Parking mode needs constant power. Your dash cam can’t run on its internal battery for more than a few minutes. It needs to be hardwired to your car’s battery with a proper power management system.

This is why professional installation matters.

A plug-in dash cam (cigarette lighter): Loses power when you turn the engine off. No parking mode. No overnight protection. Your camera is basically useless when your car is most vulnerable.

A professionally hardwired dash cam: Connected directly to your car’s battery through a hardwire kit with voltage cutoff protection. Camera stays powered 24/7. Parking mode works. Your car is protected even when you’re not in it.

The voltage cutoff is important — it automatically shuts down the camera if your battery drops below a safe level. So it won’t drain your battery flat overnight. Professional installation includes setting this up correctly.

At Hyped Up, every dash cam we install is hardwired with proper voltage management. Parking mode works from the moment you walk away from your car.

What To Look For In A Dash Cam (Honest Buying Guide)

There’s hundreds of dash cams on Amazon. Most are rubbish. Here’s what actually matters:

Resolution: 1080p minimum. 1440p (2K) is the sweet spot. 4K is available but generates massive files and the difference in usable footage is minimal for most people. You need to read number plates clearly — 1440p does that perfectly.

Night vision: Essential in London. Short days in winter, dark car parks, poorly lit streets. If the camera can’t see clearly at night, it’s useless for half the year. Look for cameras with good low-light sensors, not just IR LEDs.

GPS: Non-negotiable. Speed data, location proof, route recording. Any camera without GPS is outdated.

Parking mode: Essential if you want overnight protection. Must be combined with hardwire installation.

G-sensor: Detects impacts and automatically locks that footage so it can’t be overwritten. Every decent camera has this.

Loop recording: Camera records in continuous loops (usually 1-3 minute clips). When the memory card is full, it overwrites the oldest footage. G-sensor locked clips are protected. This means you never need to manually manage storage.

Wi-Fi/App connectivity: Lets you download footage to your phone without removing the memory card. Convenient for sending clips to insurance or police quickly.

Wide angle lens: 140-170 degrees captures the full width of the road. Narrower lenses miss things happening at the edges.

Screen vs no screen: Cameras with screens let you review footage in the car. Cameras without screens are smaller and more discreet. Personal preference — both work fine.

What we recommend: A quality 1440p front and rear camera with GPS, parking mode, Wi-Fi, and good night vision. Hardwired with proper voltage management. That covers everything.

What NOT to waste money on:

- 4K (overkill for evidence, massive files)
- Cameras with dozens of AI features you’ll never use
- Cloud subscriptions (monthly fees for storage you don’t need)
- Cheap no-brand cameras under £50 (awful quality, unreliable)
How Long Does Dash Cam Footage Last?

Depends on two things: memory card size and recording resolution.

At 1440p with a 128GB card:

- Front only: approximately 12-14 hours of continuous recording
- Front and rear: approximately 6-8 hours of continuous recording
At 1080p with a 128GB card:

- Front only: approximately 20-24 hours
- Front and rear: approximately 10-12 hours
“But what if something happened yesterday and it’s been overwritten?”

Two protections against this:

G-sensor locking: Any impact automatically locks that clip. It won’t be overwritten. If someone hits your parked car at 3am, that footage is safe even if you don’t check the camera until 6pm the next day.

Manual locking: If something happens that you want to keep, press the lock button (or tap the screen). That clip is protected from overwriting.

For most people, a 128GB card in a front and rear setup gives you a full day of footage before the oldest clips start being overwritten. That’s more than enough for daily driving.

We recommend checking your footage weekly if you want peace of mind, and immediately if you notice any damage to your car.

AirTag vs GPS Dash Cam: Are They The Same Thing?

Quick answer: No. Not even close.

AirTag:

GPS Dash Cam:

- GPS shows exact speed and location

An AirTag is a £29 Bluetooth beacon. A dash cam is a video evidence system. A GPS tracker is a professional recovery system. They do completely different things.

Can an AirTag help? Maybe slightly. Is it a security system? Absolutely not.

If you care about protecting your car properly, you need a dash cam for evidence AND a GPS tracker for recovery. Not an AirTag.
