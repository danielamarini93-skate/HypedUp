What Affects The Price Of A Car Wrap?

Six things. And understanding them explains why quotes vary so much.

1. Vehicle Type And Size (The Biggest Factor)
This isn’t just about square metres of film. A moped at £900 uses far less film than a luxury SUV at £4,995. But it’s also about complexity.

Some small cars have incredibly complex curves that take longer per panel than a big flat-sided van. A Porsche Cayman has air intakes, vents, and curves that take serious skill. A supercar at £5,800 has exotic panel shapes, carbon fibre elements, and tolerances measured in millimetres.

That’s why pricing varies by vehicle type, not just by size.

1. Film Quality (This Is Where People Get Ripped Off)
This is the biggest factor most people don’t understand.

Premium film (3M, Avery Dennison, Hexis, KPMF):

- Lasts 5-7 years
- Self-healing on minor scratches
- Consistent colour across rolls
- Easier to remove without damage
- Better conformability around curves
- UV resistant (won’t fade)
- Costs more
Budget film (no-name Chinese brands):

- Lasts 1-3 years maximum
- No self-healing
- Colour can vary between rolls (panels don’t match)
- Difficult to remove (can damage paint)
- Doesn’t conform well to curves (bubbles, wrinkles)
- Fades in UV
- Costs less
When someone quotes you £800 for a full wrap, they’re using budget film. Guaranteed. That £800 wrap will look great for about 6 months. Then the edges lift. The colour fades. Panels start looking different shades. And you’re paying again to get it removed and redone properly.

At Hyped Up, we use quality film. Because we’d rather charge you properly once than have you come back angry in six months.

1. Wrap Finish (Matte, Gloss, Satin, Special)
Standard gloss colours: Base price. Widest range of colours available.

Matte finish: Same price or slightly more. Matte looks incredible but shows fingerprints and dirt more easily. Needs specific care products.

Satin finish: Slightly more than gloss. The sweet spot between matte and gloss. Absolutely stunning on dark colours. Our most popular finish at Hyped Up right now.

Metallic/chrome: 20-40% more expensive. Chrome film is thicker, harder to work with, and requires more skill. Not every shop can do chrome well.

Colour shift/chameleon: 30-50% more expensive. Changes colour depending on viewing angle. Incredible when done well. Very technical to install.

Carbon fibre look: Similar to gloss pricing for panels/accents. Full car in carbon look is rare but possible.

Custom printed: Price varies hugely depending on design complexity. Vehicle signage and branded wraps fall into this category.

1. Complexity Of Your Vehicle
Some vehicles are a nightmare to wrap. Others are straightforward.

Easy to wrap: Cars with flat, simple panels. Golf, 3 Series, Transit van. Panels come off easily, curves are manageable.

Hard to wrap: Cars with deep recesses, complex bumpers, air vents, scoops, flared arches. Porsche 911, McLaren, wide-body kits. Takes significantly longer. That’s why supercars start from £5,800 — every panel is a challenge.

Motorcycles: Fairings, tanks, and curved surfaces need specialist skill. A super sport bike at £1,400 takes as much precision work as a car despite being physically smaller.

Hard to wrap also means: Newer cars with lots of sensors in bumpers (parking sensors, cameras). Film has to be cut precisely around these. Takes extra time and care.

1. Preparation Quality
This is where cheap shops cut corners first.

Proper preparation includes:

- Full wash and decontamination
- Clay bar treatment (removes bonded contaminants)
- IPA wipe (removes oils and residues)
- Panel inspection for chips, dents, rust (wrap doesn’t fix these — they need addressing first)
- Removal of badges, trim pieces, and door handles where possible (so film goes UNDER them, not around them)
What cheap shops do:

- Quick wash
- Maybe a wipe down
- Wrap straight over badges (looks terrible)
- Wrap around door handles (peels within weeks)
- Skip the clay bar (film doesn’t bond properly)
At Hyped Up, preparation takes a full day before we even start wrapping. That’s built into the price. Because a £2,500 wrap over a dirty surface is a £2,500 waste of money.

1. Coverage Level
Full wrap: Every external panel. Bumpers, bonnet, roof, boot, doors, wings, mirrors. Everything.

Full wrap with door jambs: Everything above PLUS the visible inner edges when doors open. This is the proper way to do it — no original colour showing anywhere. Costs 10-15% more.

Partial wrap: Selected panels only. Roof wrap, bonnet wrap, mirror caps, boot lip. Much cheaper (individual panel prices listed above).

At Hyped Up, we recommend full wrap with door jambs for colour changes. If you’re going from white to black and every time you open the door there’s a strip of white visible, it looks unfinished. Door jambs take extra time but make the whole thing look factory.

How Long Does A Car Wrap Last?

Quality wrap with proper installation: 5-7 years.

Budget wrap with rushed installation: 1-3 years maximum.

What affects longevity:

UV exposure: Cars parked outside in direct sun degrade faster. Garaged cars last longer.

Washing: Automatic car washes with spinning brushes damage wrap edges. Hand wash only.

Climate: London’s weather isn’t extreme, so wraps last well here. But acid rain and pollution can degrade cheaper films faster.

Maintenance: Proper wrap-specific cleaning products extend life. Regular maintenance prevents contaminant buildup.

Colour: Darker colours (black, dark grey) show wear earlier than lighter colours. Not because they degrade faster — they just show imperfections more.

To get maximum life from your wrap:

- Hand wash only (no automatic car washes)
- Use wrap-specific cleaning products
- Avoid pressure washing directly on edges
- Wax or ceramic coat the wrap (yes, you can ceramic coat vinyl)
- Park in shade when possible
- Address any lifting edges immediately (small fix now prevents big peel later)
Do I Need To Tell Insurance About A Wrap?

Yes. Always.

A wrap changes your vehicle’s appearance. Insurance needs to know. Most insurers charge nothing extra or a small amount (£0-20/month).

What happens if you DON’T tell insurance and have an accident:

- Could reduce payout or deny claim entirely
- You’re out thousands
What happens if you DO tell insurance:

- They note it on your policy
- Might charge £0-20/month extra
- Claim is processed normally if you have an accident
- No issues, no surprises
It’s a 5-minute phone call. Don’t risk claim denial for the sake of 5 minutes.

Common Questions About Car Wrapping

Q: How much does a car wrap cost?

A: Full wrap at Hyped Up: from £1,800 (small car) to £5,800 (supercar). Luxury SUVs from £4,995. Chrome delete: £250-500. Individual panels: from £90 (spoiler) to £450 (race stripes). Motorcycles: from £900.

Q: How long does a full wrap take?

A: Minimum 1 week for a standard car. Luxury SUVs and supercars take 2 weeks. Rushed wraps (1-2 days) mean corners are being cut — guaranteed.

Q: Will a wrap damage my paint?

A: No. A properly applied and removed wrap protects paint. The paint underneath will be in better condition than exposed paint. Only badly applied or badly removed wraps can cause damage.

Q: Can I wrap a leased car?

A: Yes. Wraps are removable. When the lease ends, we remove it and the car goes back with pristine factory paint.

Q: Do I need to tell insurance?

A: Yes. Always. Most insurers charge nothing or very little extra. Don’t risk claim denial for a 5-minute phone call.

Q: How long does a car wrap last?

A: Quality wrap with proper installation: 5-7 years. Budget wrap: 1-3 years. Hand wash only, use wrap-specific products, avoid automatic car washes.

Q: Matte or gloss — which is better?

A: Personal preference. Gloss is classic and easier to maintain. Matte is dramatic but shows fingerprints and needs specific care. Satin is the middle ground and our most popular finish right now.

Q: Can you wrap over scratches and chips?

A: Minor ones, yes — the wrap covers them. Deep scratches and chips should be repaired first or they’ll show through the film as bumps.

Q: What’s the difference between a wrap and PPF?

A: A wrap changes colour. PPF (Paint Protection Film) is transparent and protects your existing paint from chips and scratches. You can have both — PPF on high-impact areas under a colour wrap.
