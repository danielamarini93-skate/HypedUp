Why Factory Audio Is Disappointing (Even "Premium" Systems)

Here's what manufacturers do with audio:

They install the cheapest speakers that sound "acceptable." They use thin door panels that vibrate and rattle. They power everything through a weak built-in amplifier. Then they market it as a "premium sound system" and charge you £800 extra.

The reality:

Factory speakers: Paper cones, weak magnets, low power handling. They distort at volume. They have no bass below 80Hz. They sound flat and lifeless.

Factory amplifier: Usually 10-20 watts per channel. That's nothing. A decent aftermarket amp does 50-100 watts per channel minimum.

Factory wiring: Thin gauge, minimal shielding. Picks up electrical noise. That buzzing you hear at certain volumes? That's electrical interference through cheap wiring.

Factory sound deadening: Almost none. Your doors vibrate. Your boot rattles. Half the bass energy is wasted moving metal panels instead of air.

Even cars with "Harman Kardon" or "Bang & Olufsen" badges are using versions of those systems that are massively cost-reduced for automotive. They're better than base level, but they're nowhere near what a proper aftermarket system delivers.

Why Installation Matters More Than Brand

Here's the truth that audio shops don't always tell you: **a £200 speaker installed properly will sound better than a £500 speaker installed badly.**

Why? Because audio quality depends on:

The seal: Speakers need an airtight seal behind them to produce bass. Factory doors leak air everywhere. We seal them properly.

Sound deadening: Your door panel is a thin sheet of metal. When bass hits it, it vibrates and rattles. We add sound deadening material (Dynamat or similar) to stop this. Result: tighter bass, no rattles, cleaner sound.

Wiring: Proper gauge speaker wire. Shielded. No interference. No voltage drop. Clean signal from amp to speaker.

Amplifier placement: Heat management, ventilation, vibration isolation. An amp bolted directly to metal with no isolation will vibrate and potentially overheat.

Subwoofer enclosure: The box matters as much as the sub. Wrong volume = wrong sound. Too big = boomy and loose. Too small = tight but no depth. We calculate the right enclosure volume for your specific subwoofer.

Tuning: Every car's interior is different. The shape, materials, glass, seats - all affect how sound behaves. We tune the system to YOUR car. Not a generic setting.

What happens with bad installation:

- Speakers rattle against door panels (no sound deadening)
- Bass sounds boomy and undefined (wrong enclosure)
- Electrical buzzing at certain frequencies (poor wiring)
- Amplifier overheats and shuts down (bad placement)
- System cuts out randomly (loose connections)
- Battery drains overnight (improper wiring to ignition)
We've fixed dozens of bad installations from other shops. Usually costs more to fix than it would have cost to do it right the first time.

Common Questions About Car Audio

Q: Will it drain my battery?

A: Not if installed properly. We wire everything through the ignition circuit. System only draws power when the car's running. Dodgy installations that wire directly to the battery? Yeah, those drain it overnight.

Q: Will a subwoofer damage my car?

A: No. Properly installed with sound deadening, there's no damage. Without sound deadening, vibrations can loosen trim clips over time. That's why we always recommend sound deadening with any sub install.

Q: How loud will it be?

A: As loud or as quiet as you want. A good system doesn't have to be loud to sound good. It's about clarity, not volume. But yes, it CAN get loud if you want it to.

Q: Will it affect my car's warranty?

A: Speaker upgrades are generally non-invasive and won't affect warranty. Amplifier and sub installations involve additional wiring, so technically could be questioned. But we do everything reversibly - can always put it back to stock.

Q: Can I keep my factory head unit?

A: Yes. We can add an amplifier and speakers while keeping your factory stereo. Or upgrade to Pioneer/Kenwood CarPlay unit and do audio at the same time.

Q: How long does installation take?

A: Speaker swap: 2-3 hours. Speaker + amp: 4-6 hours. Full system with sub: 1-2 days. Custom boot build: 1-3 weeks.
