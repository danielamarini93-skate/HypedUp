What's Actually Destroying Your Paint (Right Now)

If you're driving in South London, your paint faces:

Road grime and tar: The Old Kent Road alone will cover your car in sticky black tar spots that eat into clear coat if left too long.

Stone chips: Every lorry, every van, every bus on London roads is kicking up debris. Your bonnet and front bumper take the worst of it.

Acid rain: London's pollution means rain is acidic. It sits on your paint, dries, and leaves etching marks that won't wash off.

UV damage: Even in the UK, UV degrades paint over years. Reds fade. Blacks oxidise. Whites yellow.

Bird droppings: Acidic. If left for more than 48 hours, they etch permanently into clear coat.

Automatic car washes: The single biggest destroyer of paint in London. Those spinning brushes are full of grit from every car that went through before yours. It's like sandpapering your paint twice a week.

Tree sap: Park under a tree in Bermondsey? That sap bonds to clear coat and requires clay bar treatment to remove.

Your paint is under constant attack. Every day. And factory clear coat is only about 2-3 microns thick. Once it's gone, it's gone.

Paint Protection Film (PPF)

What it is: A transparent, self-healing film applied directly over your paint. Physical barrier between your paint and the world.

What it does:

- Absorbs stone chips (film takes the hit, not your paint)
- Self-heals minor scratches (heat from sun or warm water repairs light scratches in the film)
- Blocks UV damage
- Resists chemical etching (bird droppings, tree sap, acid rain)
- Virtually invisible when applied properly
Options:

- Full car PPF (complete protection, every panel)
- Partial PPF (high-impact areas: bonnet, bumper, wings, mirrors, door edges)
- Bespoke coverage (you choose which panels)
Best for: New cars, expensive cars, cars you want to keep perfect. Anyone who cares about paint condition and resale value.

Real example: Customer buys a new Mercedes C-Class. Before driving it daily, brings it to us for partial PPF (bonnet, front bumper, wing mirrors, door edges). Cost: £800. Two years later, car looks brand new. No chips, no scratches, paint is perfect under the film.

Window Tinting

What it is: Tinted film applied to windows. Reduces light, heat, and UV entering the car.

What it does:

- Reduces heat inside the car (huge difference in summer)
- Blocks 99% of UV rays (protects interior from fading)
- Reduces glare (easier driving in bright conditions)
- Privacy (can't see inside from outside)
- Looks clean and premium
Legal limits (UK):

- Front windscreen: minimum 75% light transmission
- Front side windows: minimum 70% light transmission
- Rear windows: ANY darkness is legal
Options:

- Standard tint (dyed film, good value)
- Ceramic tint (premium, blocks more heat without being darker, no signal interference)
- Full rear tint (rear windows + rear windscreen)
- Full car tint (within legal limits on front)
Best for: Everyone. Seriously. Window tinting improves comfort, protects your interior, and looks better. One of the most cost-effective modifications.

The Difference Between Good and Bad Wrapping

This is where I get really passionate. Because bad wrapping is everywhere.

Signs of a bad wrap:

- Edges lifting within weeks (poor surface prep or cheap film)
- Bubbles visible in certain light (air trapped during application)
- Colour doesn't match across panels (inconsistent heating)
- Film pulling away from curves and recesses (not tucked properly)
- Visible join lines (poor panel planning)
- Film peeling around door handles and mirrors (these are the hardest bits - skip them and it shows)
Signs of a good wrap:

- Seamless appearance (you shouldn't be able to tell it's wrapped unless you look very closely)
- Film tucked into edges and recesses (not just folded over the edge)
- Consistent colour and finish across every panel
- No bubbles, no lifting, no pulling
- Curves and complex shapes handled cleanly
- Door jambs wrapped (so you don't see original colour when doors open)
At Hyped Up, we take wrapping seriously. We prep the surface properly (clay bar, IPA wipe, remove contaminants). We use quality film. We take the time to tuck every edge. We heat and post-heat properly.

A full wrap takes 3-5 days. If someone tells you they can wrap your car in a day, they're cutting corners. Guaranteed.

Common Questions

Q: How long does a wrap last?

A: Quality wrap lasts 5-7 years with proper care. Cheaper films might only last 2-3 years. We use quality film that's proven to last.

Q: Will a wrap damage my paint?

A: No. Actually the opposite - it protects it. When you remove a wrap, the paint underneath is in better condition than exposed paint. As long as the wrap is removed properly (with heat, not ripped off).

Q: How long does PPF last?

A: Quality PPF lasts 7-10 years. It's a long-term investment. Self-healing properties work for the entire lifespan.

Q: Is window tinting legal?

A: Yes, within limits. Front windscreen must allow 75% light through. Front side windows must allow 70%. Rear windows can be any darkness. We always tint within legal limits on fronts.

Q: Will tinting affect my MOT?

A: Not if it's within legal limits. We use calibrated light meters to verify compliance before you leave.

Q: How do I maintain a wrapped car?

A: Hand wash only. No automatic car washes. No high-pressure washers directly on edges. Use wrap-specific cleaning products. We give you full care instructions.

Q: Can I wrap a leased car?

A: Yes. Wraps are removable. When the lease ends, we remove the wrap, return the car in original condition. The paint underneath will be better than an unwrapped leased car.

Q: Do I need to tell insurance about a wrap?

A: Yes. Wraps change the vehicle's appearance. Tell your insurance. Most don't charge extra or charge very little. Don't risk claim denial for the sake of a 5-minute phone call.
